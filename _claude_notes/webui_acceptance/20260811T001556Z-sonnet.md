<details><summary>Answer</summary>

I'll read the extraction notes first, then look at net 125 and its surroundings.## What the 24 V reading actually proves

24 V on net 125 is the CR-BP coil-return point — it sits between the CR-BP coil's A2 terminal and the BYPASS breaker/switch, on its way back to 0V (`circuit_logic.json` → `nets[]` id `125`, members `CR-BP:A2`, `BYPASS-CB:2`). A steady 24 V there, with a high-impedance meter, means the return path from this point down to 0V is **open somewhere** — it does not tell you where. It is also consistent with the CR-BP coil itself never pulling in, since no current flows and the meter still reads full source voltage across the open coil.

Green lamps on PB1/PB2 prove the pushbutton lamp supply is present and that each contact block has physically moved — they do **not** prove the CR1 or CR2 relay contacts downstream actually closed. Likewise "BYPASS breaker closed" tells you the handle position, not that its internal switch contacts are making up — REVERSE/BYPASS 5A are DIN breakers repurposed as manual switches here (see inference 7 in `EXTRACTION_NOTES.md`), not protective devices, and their contacts can be worn or fail to make just like any switch.

## Complete candidate path

For the machine to run via the bypass route, this whole chain has to close, hot rail to 0V:

`24E-1` → `CR-BP:A1` (coil) → `CR-BP:A2` →[net 125]→ `BYPASS-CB:2` ─[BYPASS 5A switch]─ `BYPASS-CB:1` → net `120` → `CR2:14` ─[CR2 N.O. contact 11-14]─ `CR2:11` → net `121` → `CR1:14` ─[CR1 N.O. contact 11-14]─ `CR1:11` → `0V`

If that completes, CR-BP coil pulls in and its own N.O. contact `CR-BP:21-24` closes, feeding net `RUN` → `RECEPT1:3` (pin numbering inferred, not printed) to the drive cards. RUN can also be fed independently by `CR-ON:11-14` via net `110`, which is a separate path you should check too (`circuit_logic.json` → `nets[]` id `RUN`, id `110`).

## Ranked suspects

| Rank | Suspect | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CR1/CR2 series contact chain (net 121, between `CR1:14` and `CR2:11`) | Lit PB lamps don't prove these N.O. contacts actually closed — this is the domain trap on this drawing, and it's the first thing between net 120 and 0V. |
| 2 | BYPASS-CB switch contacts (`BYPASS-CB:1` to `BYPASS-CB:2`) | Handle "closed" doesn't guarantee the internal contacts are making up on a breaker used as a switch. |
| 3 | CR-BP coil open (`CR-BP:A1`–`A2`) | Would produce exactly this symptom (full 24 V at 125, no pull-in) but coil failures are less common than a worn contact — check after 1 and 2. |
| 4 | CR-BP contact `21-24` failed to close even though the coil pulled in | Only relevant if the coil is confirmed energized; would explain RUN staying dead even with 125 pulled correctly. |

## Probe procedure

1. **Confirm CR-BP coil state.** Meter across `CR-BP:A1` to `A2` (or listen/feel for pull-in). If it hasn't pulled in, the coil circuit is open somewhere below net 125 — proceed to steps 2–3. If it *has* pulled in, skip to step 4.
2. **Meter from net 120 (e.g. `BYPASS-CB:1`) to 0V**, buttons held on. 0 V here means the CR1/CR2 chain is genuinely closed — suspect moves to the BYPASS switch or CR-BP coil. Still near 24 V here means the open is in the CR1/CR2 contacts themselves (suspect #1).
3. **If step 2 shows 0 V at net 120**, meter across `BYPASS-CB:1` to `BYPASS-CB:2` with the switch closed. Any significant reading (not near 0 V) means the switch contacts aren't making up despite the handle position — replace/inspect the BYPASS 5A unit.
4. **If CR-BP coil is confirmed pulled in**, meter net `RUN` (e.g. `TB-RUN:1`) to 0V. If RUN is not near 24 V, check `CR-BP:21-24` contact continuity directly — the coil energizing does not guarantee that contact block is also good.
5. **In parallel**, check the CR-ON path: meter net `110` to 0V and `CR-ON:11-14` contact, since RUN can also be driven that way and a failure there is independent of everything above.

## Eliminating alternatives

- **CR-BP:A1 supply (net 24E-1):** already proven live by the fact you're reading 24 V downstream at net 125 — the coil's hot side is not a suspect.
- **RECEPT1:3 / drive-card wiring:** not reachable yet — you haven't established RUN is even being asserted, so there's no reason to suspect the receptacle or downstream cabling first.
- **CR-ON path via net 110/CR-SW:** net 130 (CR-SW's coil return) only completes through the downstream machine and nothing on this sheet can energize CR-SW — so if you're troubleshooting in isolation, CR-ON is a dead end regardless of bypass status. Don't chase it unless the downstream machine's infeed signal is confirmed present.
